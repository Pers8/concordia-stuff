?

| Category                     | Key feature                     | How to separate                      | Example          |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | ---------------- |
| **Matter**                   | Anything with mass and volume   | —                                    | air, rock, water |
| **Mixtures**                 | Physical blend of 2+ substances | **Physically** separable             | salt water, air  |
| ➤ **Heterogeneous**          | Non-uniform                     | filter / pick apart                  | sand + water     |

## Physical change vs. chemical change

- **Physical change** =:: particles stay the **same substance**, only their **arrangement/spacing** changes (state, size, dissolution).
- **Chemical change** =:: particles become a **new substance** because **bonds are broken/formed** (composition changes).

**Melting butter / boiling water / dissolving sugar** →:: **Physical** (same molecules, new arrangement).
**Burning butter / digesting sugar / reacting Na(s) with water** →:: **Chemical** (new substances).

**Dissolving NaCl in water:**:: still **physical** (you get solvated **Na⁺/Cl⁻**, but NaCl is recovered by evaporating water; no new compound formed).  
**Gas observed:**:: could be **physical** (boiling) **or** **chemical** (acid + carbonate → CO₂). Identify the cause.  
**Precipitation when two solutions are mixed:**:: **chemical** (new insoluble compound formed).  
**Sublimation/deposition (e.g., dry ice):**:: **physical**.  
**Rusting, tarnishing, fermentation, combustion, neutralization:**:: **chemical**.

## Chemical change = change in composition (identity)
?

|View type|What you see|Example meaning|
|---|---|---|
|**Macroscopic**|What you observe|Hydrogen burning (visible flame)|
|**Particulate**|Molecules/atoms rearrange|H₂ and O₂ bonds break → H₂O bonds form|
|**Symbolic**|Chemical equation|2 H₂ + O₂ → 2 H₂O|

A **chemical change** means:: **atoms are rearranged into new combinations**, forming **new substances** but **no atoms are gained or lost overall**. This is why we **balance equations** (mass is conserved).

**Law of Conservation of Matter:**:: atoms are rearranged, **not destroyed or created**.

## MEASUREMENTS: Uncertainty & Significant Figures

_“How many significant figures are in 0.648 ± 0.001 m?”_ →:: **3 SF.**
_“Which instrument gives the most precise measurement?”_ →:: The one with the smallest uncertainty (e.g., ± 0.001 m).
_“Why is the last digit uncertain?”_ →:: Because it’s estimated between markings.

## Comparing data sets: precision vs. accuracy

**Precision** =:: how tightly repeated measurements cluster together.
**Accuracy** =:: how close the **average** (mean) is to the **accepted/true** value.
% relative error :
?
	$$\frac{\text{Observed - Accepted}}{\text{Accepted Value}} \times 100$$
**Deviation (per trial)** =:: $|\text{observed} - \text{average}|$
**Systematic error** =:: consistent shift of all results (good precision but wrong mean) → usually a **miscalibration**.
**Random error** =:: shot-to-shot scatter (hurts precision).
