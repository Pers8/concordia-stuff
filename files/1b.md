## J.J. Thomson (1897): electrons in all atoms

What he did (cathode-ray tube)
?
- Applied a **high voltage** to metal electrodes → a **beam** (“cathode rays”) forms.
- Put an **electric field** across the beam → it **bends toward the + plate**.

What that shows
?
- The beam is made of **negatively charged particles**.
- From how much it bends, Thomson estimated a tiny **mass/charge ratio** → particles are **~1/2000 the mass of an H atom** (i.e., way lighter than atoms).
- Therefore, **electrons exist** and are **in all atoms** (universal constituents of matter).

Model he proposed
?
- **“Plum-pudding” model**: electrons embedded in a diffuse positive “pudding.”  
    → **Important:** this model is **wrong** (Rutherford later disproves it).

What they like to ask
?
- **Who found electrons?** → **Thomson** (cathode rays, e- are negative, very light).
- **Who measured electron charge qeq_eqe​?** → **Millikan** (oil-drop).
- **Who discovered the nucleus/empty space?** → **Rutherford** (gold foil).
- **Why does the ray bend to + plate?** → Particles are **negative**.
- **Evidence electrons are sub-atomic?** → **Much lighter** than H atom.

## Robert Millikan (1909): quantified charge on electron
?
Millikan **measured the exact charge of a single electron** using falling oil droplets.  
He balanced **gravity** (pulling down) with an **electric field** (pushing up) until droplets hovered.

What he did :
?
1. **Ionized air** so electrons attached to oil droplets.
2. Each droplet had **1 or more e⁻** → different total charge.
3. Adjusted the voltage until the drop floated → forces balanced.
4. From the **voltage, mass, and gravity**, calculated charge per drop.
5. All charges were **multiples of a single smallest value** → the **electron’s charge**.

**Charge on one electron:**:: $-1.60 \times 10^{-19}\ \text{C}$ (1 unit of negative charge).
**Mass of one electron:**:: $9.11 \times 10^{-28}\ \text{g}$ (using Thomson’s charge/mass ratio).


## Ernest Rutherford (1910): protons in nucleus
?
Rutherford **disproved Thomson’s plum-pudding model** using the **gold-foil experiment**, showing that atoms have a **dense, positively charged nucleus**.  

Rutherford’s gold-foil experiment :

- **Setup:** fired α-particles (He²⁺, heavy + charged) at a thin gold foil.  
- **Expectation (plum-pudding model):** α-particles should pass through with tiny deflections (positive charge spread evenly). 
- **Result:**
    - Most α-particles **passed straight through** → atom is **mostly empty space**.    
    - A few **deflected sharply** or **bounced back** → there must be a **small, dense, positive center (nucleus)**.

## Atomic symbols: atomic number & atomic mass

- **Atomic number (Z)** → number of **protons**
    - Determines the **element’s identity**. 
    - e.g. all atoms with 6 protons = **carbon**, no exceptions. 
- **Mass number (A)** → total **protons + neutrons**
    - $A = Z + n^0$
    - Neutrons add mass but no charge.   
- **Neutral atom:**
    - $p^+ = e^-$ → positive charge from protons balances negative charge from electrons.
    - Neutrons are **neutral** and don’t affect charge.

### How to read notation

Example:

$$^{12}{_6}\text{C}​$$

- **12** → mass number (p⁺ + n⁰)
    
- **6** → atomic number (p⁺ only)
    
- **Element symbol (C)** identifies the element.
    

**So:**

- p=6p = 6p=6
    
- e=6e = 6e=6 (neutral atom)
    
- n=12−6=6n = 12 - 6 = 6n=12−6=6
