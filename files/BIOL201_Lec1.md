## 1.1 Science (what it is + how it works)

- Science is:: knowledge about general truths/laws obtained and tested through the scientific method.
- Science assumes that:: observations give useful information about the universe.
- Science proposes:: natural explanations for natural phenomena.
- Science tests explanations using:: observations of the universe.
- A scientific explanation must be:: testable (you can name observations that would contradict it).
- Observations inconsistent with an explanation are evidence:: against it.
- Observations consistent with an explanation may be evidence:: for it.
- In science, we work with:: evidence, not absolute proof.

### Hypothesis vs fact vs theory

- Hypothesis is:: a statement of what might be true (a testable explanation).
- Fact is:: a hypothesis supported so strongly that it would be unreasonable to withhold provisional consent.
- Theory is:: a comprehensive explanation supported by a vast body of evidence.
- A theory is NOT:: “just a guess.”

## 1.2 Atoms and elements

- Element is:: a substance that cannot be broken down into other kinds of substances by ordinary chemical means.
- Atom is:: the smallest unit of an element that retains the element’s properties.
- Proton is:: + charge, ~1 mass unit.
- Neutron is:: 0 charge, ~1 mass unit.
- Electron is:: − charge, tiny mass.
- The number of protons determines:: the element.
- Isotopes are:: atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons.

## 1.3 Electron shells and valence (predict bonding)

- Electron shells/energy levels are:: regions where electrons are found around the nucleus (intro model).
- Valence shell is:: the outermost shell that contains one or more electrons.
- Valence electrons matter because:: they determine bonding behavior.
- “Octet rule” (rule of thumb) is:: many atoms are stable with 8 electrons in the outer shell (H is stable with 2).

### Typical bonds in bio (rule of thumb)

- Hydrogen usually forms:: 1 bond.
- Carbon usually forms:: 4 bonds.
- Nitrogen usually forms:: 3 bonds (and has 1 lone pair).
- Oxygen usually forms:: 2 bonds (and has 2 lone pairs).

## 1.4 Bonds + polarity

### Covalent

- Covalent bond is:: when two atoms share a pair of valence electrons.
- Molecule is:: two or more atoms held together by chemical bonding.
- Compound is:: a molecule containing two or more different elements.
- Emergent properties means:: compounds can have properties not “predictable” from the elements alone.

### Polarity + hydrogen bonds

- Polar molecule is:: a molecule with an uneven distribution of electric charge.
- Hydrogen bond is:: a weak attraction between polar molecules.
- Water is polar because (3 reasons)
?
- Oxygen attracts shared electrons more strongly than hydrogen  
- Oxygen becomes δ− and hydrogens become δ+  
- This allows hydrogen bonding between water molecules

### Ions + ionic

- Ion is:: an atom or molecule with a net charge.
- Ionic bond is:: attraction between oppositely charged ions.
- Crystal is:: a regular 3D lattice of atoms/ions (ex: salt).

### Classic examples (image)

- NaCl is:: ionic (Na+ and Cl− attract after electron transfer).
- H2 is:: nonpolar covalent (equal sharing).
- H2O is:: polar covalent (unequal sharing) + hydrogen bonds between molecules.

## 1.5 Mixtures

- Homogeneous mixture (solution) is:: mixture where components cannot be distinguished with a light microscope (uniform).
- Heterogeneous mixture is:: mixture where components can be distinguished (non-uniform).

## 1.6 Reactions + energy + enzymes

- Chemical reaction is:: making and breaking chemical bonds.
- Activation energy (Ea) is:: the energy barrier needed to reach the transition state.
- Exergonic reaction is:: products lower energy than reactants (ΔG < 0, overall release).
- Endergonic reaction is:: products higher energy than reactants (ΔG > 0, overall input needed).

- Enzyme is:: a catalyst that makes a chemical reaction happen more easily.
- Enzymes mainly do:: lower activation energy (Ea).
- Enzymes do NOT do:: change ΔG (start/end energies).

- Kinetic energy is:: energy of motion.
- Temperature is:: a measure of average kinetic energy of molecules.

## 1.7 Acids, bases, pH

- Acidity refers to:: concentration of hydrogen ions [H+].
- Acid is:: increases [H+] in water.
- Base is:: increases [OH−] in water.
- Water dissociation is:: $H_2O \Rightarrow \text{H+ + OH−}$ (small amount).
- pH =:: −log10[H+]  
- pH = 7:: neutral  
- pH < 7:: acidic  
- pH > 7:: basic
- Why pH changes matter is:: pH is logarithmic (1 unit = 10× change in [H+]).

## 1.8 Diffusion + osmosis

- Diffusion is:: net movement of a substance from high → low concentration.
- “Net movement” means:: random motion happens both ways, but overall trend is down the gradient until equilibrium.
- Concentration gradient is:: difference in concentration across space.
- Osmosis is:: diffusion of water across a semipermeable membrane.

### (Often tested) tonicity words

- Isotonic means:: same solute concentration → no net water movement.
- Hypotonic means:: outside has lower solute → water moves into cell.
- Hypertonic means:: outside has higher solute → water moves out of cell.

## 1.10 Lipids

- Lipids are:: nonpolar and high in chemical energy.
- Examples of lipids include:: fats, oils, waxes.
- Lipids are hydrophobic because:: they are mostly nonpolar.

## 1.11 Carbohydrates

- Carbohydrates are:: polar and high in chemical energy.
- Examples include:: sugars, starch, cellulose.

- Monosaccharide is:: single sugar unit (glucose, fructose, galactose).
- Disaccharide is:: two sugar units (sucrose, lactose, maltose).
- Polysaccharide is:: many sugar units (starch, glycogen, cellulose).

- Starch vs glycogen vs cellulose
?
- Starch: plant energy storage  
- Glycogen: animal energy storage  
- Cellulose: plant structural support

## 1.12 Proteins

- Protein is:: a folded chain of amino acids.
- Polypeptide is:: an unfolded (or not-yet-folded) amino acid chain.
- Proteins can be:: partly polar and partly nonpolar.
- Examples of proteins include:: enzymes, hormones, structural proteins.

### Protein structure levels

- Primary structure is:: amino acid sequence.
- Secondary structure is:: repeated coils/folds (ex: alpha helix, beta sheet).
- Tertiary structure is:: overall 3D folding of one polypeptide.
- Quaternary structure is:: multiple polypeptides assembled together (not always present).

- If a protein loses its shape, it usually:: loses its function.

## 1.13 Nucleic acids

- Nucleic acids include:: DNA and RNA.
- Main role of nucleic acids is:: storing and transmitting genetic information.
