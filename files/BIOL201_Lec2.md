## 2.1 Big Ideas

- Life is:: an emergent property of a particular arrangement of certain molecules.
- The basic unit of life is:: the cell.
- Unicellular means:: one cell carries out all life processes.
- Multicellular means:: many cells with specialized functions.

- Cells are small because:: they need a high surface area to volume ratio (SA:V) to exchange materials with the environment.

- Why SA:V matters?
  ?
  - Exchange happens at the surface (membrane).
  - Needs increase with volume.
  - If a cell gets too big, it can’t bring in nutrients / remove waste fast enough.

## 2.2 Cell Theory + “why cells exist”

- Cell theory:: living things are made of cells and the cell is the basic unit of life.
- Cells are the smallest “units” that can:: do all properties of life (metabolism, regulation, reproduction, etc.).

- What “true nucleus” means:: DNA is enclosed by a nuclear envelope (membrane).

## 2.3 Prokaryotic vs Eukaryotic Cells

- Prokaryotic cells are:: cells with no true nucleus (DNA in a nucleoid region).
- Eukaryotic cells are:: cells with a true nucleus (DNA inside nuclear envelope).

- Prokaryote vs Eukaryote (4 differences for both) ?
  - Prokaryote: no nucleus, no membrane-bound organelles, smaller, simpler internal organization.
  - Eukaryote: nucleus + membrane-bound organelles, larger, compartmentalized.

![[BIOL201_Lec2_prokaryote_annotated.png|450]]

### Prokaryotic cell structures

- Nucleoid is:: the region where the main circular chromosome (DNA) is located (not membrane-bound).
- Ribosomes do:: the protein synthesis.
- Cell wall does:: shape + protection + prevents bursting (and helps prevent dehydration).
- Capsule does:: adhesion + protection (present in some bacteria).
- Flagella function is:: locomotion (movement).
- Pili function is:: DNA transfer during conjugation.
- Fimbriae function is:: attachment to host surfaces/cells.
- Plasmid is:: small extra circular DNA (often useful genes; optional).

- Typical prokaryote size range is:: ~0.1 to 5.0 μm.
- Typical eukaryote size range is:: ~10 to 100 μm.

## 2.4 Eukaryotic Cells = Organelles + Compartmentalization

### The core logic
- A membrane-bound organelle is helpful because:: it creates a separate “room” with a different environment (pH, enzymes, ions).
- Compartmentalization allows:: efficiency + control (reactions don’t interfere).

## 2.5 Animal Cell Organelles (what to know)

![[BIOL201_Lec2_animal_cell_mito_annotated.png|500]]

### Nucleus + Ribosomes
- Nucleus function is:: stores DNA + controls gene expression (via RNA).
- Nucleolus function is:: makes ribosomal subunits (rRNA + proteins).
- Ribosomes function is:: protein synthesis (translation).

### Endomembrane system (the “shipping system”)
- Endomembrane system is:: a network that modifies, packages, and transports proteins/lipids.

- The endomembrane system includes
?
  - nuclear envelope
  - ER (rough + smooth)
  - Golgi
  - vesicles
  - lysosomes
  - plasma membrane

- Rough ER does:: modifies proteins + makes proteins destined for secretion/membranes.
- Smooth ER does:: lipid/steroid synthesis + detox + Ca²⁺ storage.
- Golgi apparatus does:: sorts + tags + packages proteins/lipids for where they need to go.
- Vesicles do:: transport or store materials.
- Lysosomes do:: digestion + recycling (break down macromolecules, old organelles, pathogens).

### Mitochondria
- Mitochondria function is:: cellular respiration → ATP (most ATP in the cell).
- Cristae are:: folds of the inner mitochondrial membrane (increase surface area for ATP-making).

## 2.6 Plant Cell Organelles (what’s different)

![[BIOL201_Lec2_plant_cell_chloro_annotated.png|700]]

- Plant cells have (vs animal cells)
?
  - Cell wall (cellulose)
  - Chloroplasts
  - Large central vacuole

- Cell wall (plants) is made of:: cellulose.
- Cell wall function is:: protection + structural support + shape.
- Chloroplast function is:: photosynthesis.
- Central vacuole function is:: storage + can expand (supports growth) + helps maintain pressure (turgor).

- Thylakoids/grana are:: internal membrane stacks in chloroplasts (site of light reactions).
- Stroma is:: fluid region around thylakoids (Calvin cycle occurs here in deeper detail later).

# 2.9 Membranes: Structure + Fluid Mosaic Model

![[BIOL201_Lec2_membrane_fluid_mosaic_annotated.png|750]]

## Core structure
- Plasma membrane is:: a phospholipid bilayer with embedded proteins (selectively permeable).
- Phospholipids are amphipathic:: they have a hydrophilic head + hydrophobic tails.
- Hydrophilic means:: water-loving.
- Hydrophobic means:: water-hating.

- Why bilayer forms automatically (2 reasons)
?
  - Heads face water (outside + cytoplasm).
  - Tails hide from water (inside core).

## Fluid Mosaic Model (key phrase)
- Fluid mosaic model means:: membrane components move laterally and form a “mosaic” of lipids + proteins + carbohydrates.

### Membrane components (functions)
- Cholesterol does:: stabilizes membrane + adjusts fluidity.
- Glycoprotein is:: protein + carbohydrate chain (cell recognition/signaling).
- Glycolipid is:: lipid + carbohydrate chain (cell recognition/signaling).

### Membrane proteins (high yield)
- Integral protein is:: embedded in membrane; often spans it.
- Peripheral protein is:: attached to one side of membrane (inside or outside).

- Channel protein is:: a hollow pathway for specific solutes to cross.
- Carrier protein is:: binds solute and changes shape to move it across.

# 2.10 Transport Across Membranes (must know)

![[BIOL201_Lec2_transport_summary.png|750]]

## Gradients
- Concentration gradient is:: area of high concentration next to low concentration.
- Diffusion is:: net movement from high → low concentration (down gradient).

## Passive transport (no ATP)
- Passive transport is:: movement across a membrane without energy input (down gradient).
- Simple diffusion is best for:: small nonpolar molecules (O2, CO2).
- Osmosis is:: diffusion of water across a semipermeable membrane.

- Facilitated diffusion is:: diffusion down gradient using membrane proteins (channels/carriers).
- Aquaporin is:: a channel protein that allows water through very fast.

## Active transport (energy required)
- Active transport is:: movement against a gradient (low → high), requiring energy.
- Pump is:: active transport protein that works against electrochemical gradients.
- Primary active transport is:: directly uses ATP (ex: ion pumps).
- Secondary active transport is:: uses an existing gradient as energy (symport/antiport).

- Symporter is:: moves two substances same direction.
- Antiporter is:: moves two substances opposite directions.

## Bulk transport (vesicles)
- Endocytosis is:: bringing material into the cell using vesicles.
- Exocytosis is:: releasing material out of the cell when vesicles fuse with membrane.

- Phagocytosis is:: “cell eating” (large particles, cells).
- Pinocytosis is:: “cell drinking” (fluid + small molecules).
- Receptor-mediated endocytosis is:: specific uptake using membrane receptors.

# 2.11 Osmolarity + Tonicity (water movement)

- Osmolarity is:: total amount of solute dissolved in a given amount of solution.
- Tonicity describes:: how a solution affects a cell’s water balance (net water movement).

- Isotonic means:: same osmolarity inside/outside → no net water movement.
- Hypertonic means:: outside has higher osmolarity → water leaves cell.
- Hypotonic means:: outside has lower osmolarity → water enters cell.

## What happens to cells?

- Animal cell in hypotonic solution 
?
- Water enters → swelling → may lyse (burst).

- Animal cell in hypertonic solution 
?
- Water leaves → shrivels (crenates).

- Plant cell in hypotonic solution 
?
- Water enters → turgid (firm) because wall prevents bursting.

- Plant cell in hypertonic solution 
?
- Water leaves → plasmolysis (membrane pulls away from wall).

- Plasmolysis is:: membrane detaches from cell wall when plant cell is in a hypertonic solution.
