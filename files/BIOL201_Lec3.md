# BIOL 201 — Lecture 3: Cellular Respiration + Photosynthesis
#biol201/lec3

> Goal of this lecture: understand how cells **convert energy** between forms:
> - **Food energy (glucose)** → **ATP** (cellular respiration)
> - **Light energy** → **sugars** (photosynthesis)

---

## 3.1 Core vocabulary (high yield)

- Metabolism is:: all chemical reactions in an organism.
- Metabolic pathway is:: a sequence of connected reactions.
- Enzyme is:: a protein that speeds up a specific reaction.
- ATP (adenosine triphosphate) is:: the main “spendable” energy molecule in cells.
- ADP is:: lower-energy form of ATP (missing one phosphate).

- NADH / FADH2 / NADPH are:: high-energy electron carriers (reduced forms).
- NAD+ / FAD+ / NADP+ are:: low-energy electron carriers (oxidized forms).

## 3.2 Redox + electron carriers (must know)

- Oxidation is:: loss of electrons (often loss of H).
- Reduction is:: gain of electrons (often gain of H).
- A molecule that donates electrons is a:: reducing agent.
- A molecule that accepts electrons is an:: oxidizing agent.

- NAD+ is:: oxidized electron carrier.
- NADH is:: reduced electron carrier (carries high-energy electrons).
- FAD is:: oxidized electron carrier.
- FADH2 is:: reduced electron carrier.
- NADPH is mainly used for:: building (anabolic) reactions + photosynthesis.
- NADH is mainly used for:: energy extraction (catabolic) reactions.

## 3.3 Cellular respiration — big picture

![[BIOL201_Lec3_respiration_overview_annotated.png|500]]

- Cellular respiration is:: extracting energy from food (glucose) to make ATP.
- Aerobic respiration requires:: oxygen (O2).
- The main ATP-producing stage is:: oxidative phosphorylation (ETC + chemiosmosis).

- Where glycolysis occurs (eukaryotes) is:: cytosol.
- Where citric acid cycle occurs (eukaryotes) is:: mitochondrial matrix.
- Where ETC occurs (eukaryotes) is:: inner mitochondrial membrane.

- Oxygen’s role in respiration is:: final electron acceptor in the ETC (forms water).

## 3.4 Glycolysis (cytosol)

![[BIOL201_Lec3_glycolysis_annotated.png|500]]

- Glycolysis is:: breakdown of glucose into 2 pyruvate (3C each).
- Glycolysis occurs in:: cytosol (virtually all cells do it).
- Net ATP from glycolysis (per glucose) is:: 2 ATP.
- NADH made in glycolysis (per glucose) is:: 2 NADH.
- The “net” is 2 ATP because:: 4 ATP made − 2 ATP invested.

- Substrate-level phosphorylation is:: making ATP directly by transferring a phosphate from an intermediate to ADP.

## 3.5 Citric acid cycle (Krebs) + pyruvate processing

![[BIOL201_Lec3_CAC_annotated.png|500]]

- Citric acid cycle is:: cyclic pathway that extracts energy and releases CO2.
- The main purpose of the citric acid cycle is:: load electron carriers (NADH, FADH2) for the ETC.

**High-yield outputs (match your lecture diagram):**
- From 1 pyruvate (including conversion before the cycle), you get:: 4 NADH + 1 FADH2 + 1 ATP + CO2 released.

## 3.6 Oxidative phosphorylation (ETC + chemiosmosis)

![[BIOL201_Lec3_ETC_chemiosmosis_annotated.png|700]]

- Oxidative phosphorylation is:: using energy from NADH/FADH2 to build an H+ gradient that powers ATP synthase.
- ETC (electron transport chain) is:: a chain of carriers that pass electrons step-by-step.
- Chemiosmosis is:: ATP production powered by H+ moving down its gradient through ATP synthase.
- ATP synthase is:: enzyme that makes ATP using the proton gradient.

- NADH enters the ETC:: early (more energy).
- FADH2 enters the ETC:: later (less energy).

**Rule-of-thumb ATP yield (as given in your terms):**
- NADH →:: ~2.5 ATP
- FADH2 →:: ~1.5 ATP

- The final electron acceptor in aerobic respiration is:: O2.
- If O2 is missing, the ETC:: stops (can’t dump electrons), so NADH builds up and NAD+ runs out.

## 3.7 Fermentation (anaerobic backup)

![[BIOL201_Lec3_fermentation_annotated.png|700]]

- Fermentation is:: pathway that changes pyruvate using NADH to regenerate NAD+ (no O2).
- The MAIN purpose of fermentation is:: regenerate NAD+ so glycolysis can continue.
- Fermentation is much less efficient than aerobic respiration because:: it only keeps glycolysis running (no ETC ATP payoff).

- Lactic acid fermentation produces:: lactate (lactic acid) (common in animals/bacteria).
- Alcohol fermentation produces:: ethanol + CO2 (common in yeast).

- ATP gained when relying on fermentation is:: 2 ATP per glucose (from glycolysis only).

## 3.8 Light basics (why wavelength matters)

![[BIOL201_Lec3_em_spectrum_annotated.png|700]]

- Wavelength is:: distance between repeating points on a wave.
- Wavelength and energy are:: inversely related (shorter wavelength = higher energy).
- Visible light is:: the band of light pigments mainly use for photosynthesis.

- Pigment is:: molecule that absorbs visible light.
- Pigments absorb only:: specific wavelengths (not all colors).

## 3.9 Photosynthesis — big picture

![[BIOL201_Lec3_photosynthesis_overview_annotated.png|700]]

- Photosynthesis is:: using light energy to build carbohydrates from CO2 and H2O.
- The overall inputs of photosynthesis are:: CO2 + H2O + light.
- The overall outputs of photosynthesis are:: carbohydrates (sugars) + O2.

- Photosynthesis happens in:: chloroplasts (eukaryotes like plants/algae).
- Light reactions location is:: thylakoid membrane.
- Calvin cycle location is:: stroma.

## 3.10 Light reactions (make ATP + NADPH)

![[BIOL201_Lec3_light_reactions_annotated.png|700]]

- Light reactions are:: reactions that capture light energy to make ATP + NADPH.
- Water is split during light reactions to:: replace electrons and release O2.
- Oxygen (O2) released in photosynthesis comes from:: water (H2O).

- Photosystem II (PSII) does:: absorbs light + starts electron flow; water splitting happens here.
- Photosystem I (PSI) does:: absorbs light again + helps make NADPH.

- The proton (H+) gradient forms because:: ETC pumps H+ into the thylakoid lumen.
- Photophosphorylation is:: ATP production powered by light-driven electron transport + chemiosmosis.

## 3.11 Calvin cycle (use ATP + NADPH to make sugar)

![[BIOL201_Lec3_calvin_cycle_annotated.png|700]]

- Calvin cycle is:: light-independent reactions that fix CO2 into organic molecules.
- RuBisCO is:: enzyme that catalyzes CO2 fixation onto RuBP.
- RuBP is:: 5-carbon molecule that accepts CO2 (starting point of the cycle).
- G3P is:: 3-carbon sugar that can leave the cycle and help form glucose/other carbs.

- The 3 phases of the Calvin cycle are:: carbon fixation → reduction → regeneration.
- Reduction phase uses:: ATP + NADPH to turn fixed carbon into G3P.
- Regeneration phase does:: rebuild RuBP so the cycle continues.

## 3.12 Respiration vs photosynthesis (exam-style comparisons)

- Cellular respiration overall goal is:: break down sugar to make ATP.
- Photosynthesis overall goal is:: build sugar using light energy.

- In respiration, O2 is used to:: accept electrons at the end of the ETC.
- In photosynthesis, O2 is produced when:: water is split in light reactions.

- NADH is mainly linked to:: respiration (energy extraction).
- NADPH is mainly linked to:: photosynthesis (reducing power for building sugar).

- Mitochondria are the main site of:: aerobic ATP production (oxidative phosphorylation).
- Chloroplasts are the main site of:: photosynthesis (light reactions + Calvin cycle).
