## 4.1 Vocabulary

- Cell cycle is:: the repeating sequence a cell goes through to grow and divide.
- Interphase is:: the “work + prepare” part of the cell cycle (before division).
- Mitotic (M) phase is:: division phase (mitosis + cytokinesis).

- Chromosome is:: a DNA molecule (with proteins) that carries genes.
- Chromatid is:: one copy of a duplicated chromosome (sister chromatids are identical).
- Centromere is:: the region that holds sister chromatids together.

- Mitosis is:: nuclear division that produces **2 identical nuclei**.
- Cytokinesis is:: cytoplasm division that produces **2 separate cells**.

---

## 4.2 Big picture: what happens in the cell cycle?

![[BIOL201_Lec4_cell_cycle_table.png|350]]

- Interphase includes:: G1 + S + G2.
- G1 is:: cell growth + normal cell functions.
- S phase is:: DNA replication (chromosomes get duplicated).
- G2 is:: more growth + final preparation for division.

- After S phase, each chromosome is made of:: **two sister chromatids** (still counted as one chromosome until they separate).

## 4.3 Mitosis (ANIMAL cell)

> Use this order: **Interphase → Prophase → Prometaphase → Metaphase → Anaphase → Telophase → Cytokinesis**

### Interphase (animal)
![[BIOL201_Lec4_interphase_animal.png|350]]
- In interphase, DNA is:: not visibly condensed (chromatin form).
- The cell is mainly doing:: growing + copying DNA (S phase) + preparing to divide.

### Prophase (animal)
![[BIOL201_Lec4_prophase_animal.png|350]]
- In prophase, chromosomes:: condense (become visible).
- The nucleolus:: disappears (disperses).

### Prometaphase (animal)
![[BIOL201_Lec4_prometaphase_animal.png|350]]
- In prometaphase, the nuclear envelope:: breaks down.
- Spindle fibers:: begin attaching to chromosomes (at centromere region).

### Metaphase (animal)
![[BIOL201_Lec4_metaphase_animal.png|350]]
- In metaphase, chromosomes:: line up in the middle (metaphase plate).

### Anaphase (animal)
![[BIOL201_Lec4_anaphase_animal.png|350]]
- In anaphase, sister chromatids:: separate and move to opposite poles.
- After separation, each chromatid becomes a:: chromosome (now independent).

### Telophase (animal)
![[BIOL201_Lec4_telophase_animal.png|350]]
- In telophase, chromosomes:: begin to decondense.
- Nuclear envelopes:: reform around each set of chromosomes.

### Cytokinesis (animal) — cleavage furrow
![[BIOL201_Lec4_cytokinesis_animal_cleavage_furrow.png|350]]
- Animal cytokinesis happens by:: a **cleavage furrow** (membrane pinches inward).
- Outcome of mitosis (overall) is:: **2 genetically identical diploid (2n) daughter cells** (same DNA as parent cell).

- Key plant difference is cytokinesis because:: plant cells have a rigid cell wall.

### Cytokinesis (plant) — cell plate
![[BIOL201_Lec4_cytokinesis_plant_cell_plate.png|350]]
- Plant cytokinesis happens by:: a **cell plate** forming down the middle.
- The cell plate forms from:: vesicles that fuse in the center.
- The cell plate becomes:: the new cell wall + separates the two cells.

## 4.5 Quick: Mitosis vs Meiosis (what to say fast)

- Mitosis makes:: 2 identical cells (usually for growth/repair).
- Meiosis makes:: gametes (sex cells) with half the chromosomes.

---

## 4.6 Meiosis I (focus: homologous pairs separate)

### Prophase I
![[BIOL201_Lec4_meiosisI_prophaseI.png|350]]
- In prophase I, chromosomes:: condense.
- Homologous chromosomes:: pair up (form homologous pairs).
- Nuclear envelope:: breaks down and spindle begins to form.

### Metaphase I
![[BIOL201_Lec4_meiosisI_metaphaseI.png|350]]
- In metaphase I, homologous pairs:: line up at the metaphase plate.

### Anaphase I
![[BIOL201_Lec4_meiosisI_anaphaseI.png|350]]
- In anaphase I, homologous pairs:: split and move to opposite ends.
- Important: in meiosis I, what separates is:: **homologous chromosomes** (NOT sister chromatids).

### Telophase I
![[BIOL201_Lec4_meiosisI_telophaseI.png|350]]
- In telophase I, nuclear envelopes may:: re-form.
- Cytokinesis:: begins (cell starts splitting).

## 4.7 Sexual life cycles (where meiosis happens)

### Typical animals (diplontic life cycle)
![[BIOL201_Lec4_life_cycle_animals.png|300]]
- Multicellular organism is:: **2n**.
- Meiosis produces:: **gametes (n)**.
- Fertilization makes:: **zygote (2n)**.
- Zygote grows by:: mitosis (cell cycle) into the multicellular 2n organism.

### Most fungi + some algae (haplontic life cycle)
![[BIOL201_Lec4_life_cycle_fungi_some_algae.png|300]]
- Multicellular organism is mainly:: **n**.
- Fertilization makes:: **zygote (2n)** (short stage).
- Meiosis happens in the zygote to make:: **spores (n)**.
- Spores grow by mitosis into:: multicellular **n** organism.

### Plants + some algae (alternation of generations)
![[BIOL201_Lec4_life_cycle_plants_some_algae.png|350]]
- There is a multicellular **2n** stage and a multicellular **n** stage.
- Meiosis in the 2n stage produces:: **spores (n)**.
- Spores grow into:: multicellular **n** stage.
- Fertilization produces:: **zygote (2n)** which grows into multicellular 2n stage.

## 4.8 Genetics basics (definitions you actually use)

- Gene is:: a segment of DNA that affects a trait.
- Allele is:: different version of a gene (ex: P vs p).
- Genotype is:: allele combination (PP, Pp, pp).
- Phenotype is:: observable trait (purple vs white).

- Dominant allele is:: expressed when at least one copy is present (P_).
- Recessive allele is:: expressed only when two copies are present (pp).

- Homozygous is:: two same alleles (PP or pp).
- Heterozygous is:: two different alleles (Pp).

---

## 4.9 Punnett square (how to read the result)

![[BIOL201_Lec4_punnett_square.png|350]]

- Punnett square is:: a grid that predicts genotype outcomes from a cross.
- Each box represents:: a possible offspring genotype.

### Genotype → phenotype example (3:1)
![[BIOL201_Lec4_genotype_phenotype_3to1.png|400]]

- If P is dominant (purple) and p is recessive (white), then:
  - PP phenotype is:: purple
  - Pp phenotype is:: purple
  - pp phenotype is:: white
- A 3:1 phenotype ratio usually comes from:: **Pp × Pp**.
